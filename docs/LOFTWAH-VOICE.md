One voice for the games, the software, the music and the posts. Four dialects, because a coin-op cabinet and a changelog should not sound the same.

This document is written in the voice it describes. If a line here sounds like a brand talking, it is wrong.

## 1. The voice in thirty seconds

> **A working craftsman talking to a friend: specific, dry, warm, and allergic to hype.**

- **Dry, not dead.** Understatement is the default. The joke lands sideways or not at all.
- **Exact.** Real nouns, real numbers, real constraints. Jargon only when it buys precision.
- **Warm, not soft.** Inclusive by default, never punching down. Neurodivergence and mental health are ordinary facts of life, not tragedy or quirk.
- **The format does the work.** Liner notes, a cabinet, a field guide, a changelog. No mascot voice outside a game.

**The one-sentence test:** would you say this out loud to a mate, without changing a word?

If yes, keep it. If it needs a "brand voice" to justify it, cut it.

## 3. The four pillars

### 3.1 Dry, not dead

Understate. Let the work be the boast.

- **Sounds like:** "It works. That took longer than it should have."
- **Never sounds like:** "We're thrilled to announce..."
- One joke per paragraph, maximum. Two is a routine.
- Exclamation marks are for arcade cabinets, not paragraphs.
- If a sentence can lose its adjective, lose it.

### 3.2 Exact

Specificity is the whole personality. "Five thousand logo files" is a fact. "A vast library" is a smell.

- Prefer a number, a name or a material to an adjective.
- Say what a thing does, then stop. The reader will infer the benefit.
- Technical language is welcome when the audience shares it. Define it when they don't.
- Never use "AI-powered" as a boast. Say what the AI does, or leave it out.

### 3.3 Warm, not soft

The warmth is in what you notice, not in what you gush about.

- Write to one person, not a segment.
- Jokes point at systems, yourself, or the absurdity of the brief. Never at the reader, beginners, or anyone with less power than you.
- Neurodivergence, anxiety and depression are written about from familiarity, not pity. Never "suffers from", "special needs", "brave" or "inspirational".
- "People" and "players" beat "users". "Customers" is fine when you mean money.
- You can be wrong out loud. "I got this wrong" is in voice. "We apologise for any inconvenience" is not.

### 3.4 The format does the work

The format carries the joke and the proof.

- Fastfingaz is a white-label 12-inch, so the copy reads like liner notes and run-out etchings.
- Asset Hunter is a catalogue, so the copy reads like a museum label with a rights statement.
- Bubbles is a cabinet, so the copy is three words and a start button.
- LoftwahFM is a crate, so the copy is a track credit and a BPM.
- Never import a costume from somewhere else. No fake rap slang, no pirate voice, no "hello fellow gamers".

## 5. Language rules

**Sentences**

- Mostly under 20 words. One idea per sentence.
- Vary length deliberately. A short sentence after a long one is the rhythm of the voice.
- Read it aloud. If you run out of breath, cut it.

**Case and punctuation**

- Sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Lowercase is a social register. Keep it out of product UI and store copy.
- Full stops, not dashes, for the punchline. No em dashes. A full stop is almost always better.
- No ellipsis for suspense. "Loading..." is fine. "Something big is coming..." is not.
- No exclamation marks in product, docs or store copy. One is allowed in an arcade moment, and only one.

**Numbers and claims**

- Exact beats round. "40 treatments from 5,000 files" beats "thousands of assets".
- If you can't prove it, don't claim it.
- No fake urgency, no invented testimonials, no "trusted by thousands".

**Emoji**

- Social: zero or one. It should add tone, not cover a missing sentence.
- Product, docs and store: none.

**Hashtags**

- Zero to three, lowercase, descriptive. `#beats` is fine. `#fyp #viral #foryou` is reach-bait and off-voice.

**Swearing**

- Occasional, Australian, never in product UI, store copy or support.
- It can express frustration at a system. It never lands on a person.

**Spelling**

- Australian English: *colour, organise, licence* (noun), *license* (verb), *program*.
- Metric first. If you give both, give metric first.

**Names are written exactly like this**

| Name | Correct | Never |
|---|---|---|
| House / maker | Loftwah | Loft Wah, loftwah |
| Music and social handle | beatsmiff | Beatsmiff, beat smiff |
| Music project | LoftwahFM | Loftwah FM, Loftwah Fm |
| Typing instrument | Fastfingaz | Fast Fingaz, fastfingaz |
| Discovery engine | Asset Hunter | AssetHunter, asset hunter |
| Game | Bubbles | bubbles |

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| build, make, ship, fix | solution, empower, leverage |
| instrument, tool, kit | platform, ecosystem |
| possibility, technique, pattern | revolutionary, game-changing |
| original, hand-made, no samples | cutting-edge, next-gen |
| plain, honest, straight | seamless, frictionless |
| round, run, take, pack | experience, journey |
| players, people | users, audience (when you mean people) |
| start, try, hear, open | unlock, elevate, supercharge |
| quiet, small, careful | hustle, grind, crush it |

Two more:

- **"Content"** is what a feed eats. Say *video*, *track*, *post* or *writing*.
- **"Passionate"** is a shrug with a suit on. Show the work instead.

## 7. How the humour works

The corpus is 62% funny, but almost none of it is a joke in the setup-and-punchline sense. It is recognition: a precise description of something the reader has felt and never said.

Five moves that fit the voice:

1. **The honest aside.** "It's not finished. It's due."
2. **The exact number.** "Three bubbles. One angle you didn't see."
3. **The anti-climax.** "We rebuilt the renderer. It looks the same, but it no longer lies."
4. **The format joke.** "SIDE A / COMPOSITION. SIDE B / TRANSCRIPTION."
5. **The quiet close.** End on the plain fact, not the sell.

What the voice does not do:

- Punch down, or punch at all.
- Use sarcasm with strangers. Dry is not the same as mean.
- Do "random". Absurdity needs a straight face and a reason.
- Explain the joke.
- Use memes it doesn't understand.

## 8. One voice, four dialects

### Fastfingaz: liner notes

The instrument is the source of truth. The copy is a sleeve.

- **Sounds like:** "FF-001 / 120 WPM / STEREO." "ORIGINAL WORDS. NO SAMPLES." "RUN-OUT: THINK FAST / TYPE SLOW."
- **Never:** "Unleash your inner writer." "AI-powered creativity."
- **Error state:** "The take didn't record. Your words are still here."
- **CTA:** "Cut a take"

### Asset Hunter: field guide

Precise, rights-aware, unexcited. A catalogue that respects the reader.

- **Sounds like:** "Five thousand logo files. Forty ideas." "Possibility is not permission." "Every example states where it came from."
- **Never:** "Discover endless inspiration." "Your creative superpower."
- **Empty state:** "Nothing catalogued here yet."
- **CTA:** "Open the catalogue"

### Bubbles: cabinet

Short, bright, physical. Every line has to fit on a cabinet.

- **Sounds like:** "Three bubbles. One shot. Mind the angle." "Board's full. Again?" "Press start."
- **Never:** "Embark on a bubbly adventure." Any sentence with a semicolon.
- **Game over:** "That's the board."
- **CTA:** "Start a round"

### LoftwahFM and beatsmiff: crate

Track credits, run-out etchings, a dry caption. The music does the talking.

- **Sounds like:** "Need beats?" "Beats from the back room." "SIDE A: 'If You Fuck With Loftwah'. 92 BPM. No samples."
- **Never:** "New heat dropping." "Link in bio, let's grow together."
- **Caption:** "Pippa has opinions about breakfast timing."
- **CTA:** "Hear the pack"

**The test for a new product:** which object is it? A sleeve, a catalogue, a cabinet, a crate. Write from the object, not from the category.

## 10. Before and after

**Store description**

- Before: "An addictive puzzle adventure that will blow your mind!"
- After: "A coin-op puzzle game about three bubbles, one launcher, and the angle you didn't see."

**Landing page hero**

- Before: "Revolutionary AI-powered writing tool that unlocks your creativity."
- After: "A typing instrument for original words. It measures how fast you think, and nothing leaves your machine."

**Changelog**

- Before: "We're excited to announce a major update with exciting new features!"
- After: "0.4.0: renamed to Fastfingaz. Same instrument, better label."

**Support reply**

- Before: "We apologise for any inconvenience caused."
- After: "That's our fault. Here's the fix, and here's what we changed so it doesn't happen again."

**Social caption**

- Before: "New video!! Check it out!!"
- After: "Pippa wants food early."

**CTA**

- Before: "Get Started"
- After: "Try a take"

**Error toast**

- Before: "Something went wrong."
- After: "The save didn't land. Your work is still open. Try again."

**About page opening**

- Before: "Loftwah is a passionate team building innovative solutions at the intersection of music and technology."
- After: "I make games, software and beats. Mostly in that order, depending on the week."

## 11. Channel playbooks

### Game store page

1. One concrete line about what the player does.
2. One line about the twist or constraint.
3. Three bullets: what's in it, how long it takes, what it costs.
4. No genre salad. "Puzzle" is enough.

### Software landing page

1. What it is, in a sentence a non-user can repeat.
2. The constraint that makes it different: local, no server, no samples, rights-aware.
3. A screenshot caption that says something the screenshot doesn't.
4. One CTA, repeated once at the bottom, not five times.

### Docs and changelog

- Docs explain. They don't sell. Second person, present tense.
- Changelog entries lead with the change, then the reason. "Fixed: export dropped the last frame. It now counts frames the way you expect."

### Social

- The caption adds a thought. It does not describe the video.
- One sentence is a complete caption. Two is generous.
- Ask a real question only if you want a real answer.
- Reply to people the way you'd reply in a group chat: short, specific, no brand voice.

### Music release

- Format: `SIDE A / title / BPM / credit`. No mood paragraphs.
- Say what it isn't: "No samples." That's a promise the audience can check.

## 12. Guardrails

- **Never punch down.** Not at beginners, not at customers, not at other creators.
- **Neurodivergence is not a bit.** Write from experience, not diagnosis. No "on the spectrum" as a joke, no "everyone's a little ADHD".
- **Mental health:** describe the ordinary texture. The flat day, the spiral, the small win. No hotline copy unless it's a real crisis resource.
- **Accessibility:** plain language is the default, not a downgrade. Alt text describes the thing, not the vibe.
- **No invented proof.** No fake reviews, no rounded-up user counts, no "trusted by".
- **No borrowed culture.** Reference formats, not costumes. That is already the rule in Fastfingaz, and it applies everywhere.
- **The voice can be quiet.** Not every post needs a hook, a lesson or a CTA. Some just need to be true.

---

## 13. The checklist

Before anything ships:

1. Would I say this out loud to a mate?
2. Is there one specific noun or number doing real work?
3. Does it try to impress, or does it explain?
4. Is the joke pointing up or sideways, never down?
5. Could a competitor say this? If yes, cut it.
6. Does it sound like a person who ships?
7. Read it aloud. Does it run out of breath?

If it passes all seven, it's in voice.
