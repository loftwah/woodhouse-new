# Design system

## Direction

The Steward's Office: archival editorial craft, private-club restraint and modern engineering evidence. The website should feel like a working institution with a long memory.

## Materials and colour

- Ink and charcoal carry the work.
- Warm paper and ivory carry long-form reading.
- Oxidised brass marks provenance and emphasis.
- British green marks qualified work.
- Oxblood marks incidents and unresolved risk.
- Signal amber is reserved for a decision or a held gate.

Colour never carries state by itself.

## Composition vocabulary

- Stage: a quiet, asymmetric introduction with a clear focal point.
- Ledger: chronological evidence with explicit dates and sources.
- Dossier: long-form project and incident material.
- Plate: a labelled technical diagram with a text alternative.
- Marginal note: provenance, review date and status qualification.
- Office: an editorial space for contact and published conversations.

## Typography

Use a high-contrast system serif for titles and a familiar system sans-serif for navigation, data and body copy. Monospaced text is reserved for identifiers and compact evidence labels. Body measure stays readable; desktop width is used for marginalia and diagrams rather than longer lines.

## Interaction

Motion is brief, purposeful and removed under reduced-motion preferences. Hover may add detail but never hides essential information. Navigation, disclosures and external actions work by keyboard and touch.

## Responsive intent

The narrow layout is composed independently, not reduced from the desktop. Data plates reflow into labelled lists. No page relies on hover. Touch targets remain comfortable and the document has no horizontal overflow.

## Review questions

1. What should a visitor notice first?
2. What competes with that focus?
3. Can a claim be followed to its provenance?
4. Would the page still feel like Woodhouse without the wordmark?
5. Would showing the page to a strong engineering organisation strengthen the work?
